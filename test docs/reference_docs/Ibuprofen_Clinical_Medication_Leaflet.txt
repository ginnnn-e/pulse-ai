IBUPROFEN ORAL TABLETS & SUSPENSION (400mg)
Patient Information Leaflet — PulseAI Clinical Reference Library
================================================================================

1. WHAT IS IBUPROFEN AND WHAT IS IT USED FOR?
Ibuprofen belongs to a group of medicines called Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory 
Drugs (NSAIDs). It works by inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2) enzymes, 
reducing the production of prostaglandins that cause pain, fever, and inflammation.
It is indicated for:
- Relief of acute mild to moderate pain (headache, toothache, muscle aches, back pain)
- Symptomatic treatment of joint sprains, tendonitis, and soft tissue inflammation
- Reduction of fever associated with common respiratory infections
- Management of primary dysmenorrhea (menstrual cramping)

2. DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
- Adults and adolescents over 12 years: Take one 400mg tablet every 4 to 6 hours 
  as needed with water.
- Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 1200mg (3 tablets) in any 24-hour period 
  unless specifically prescribed by a physician.
- Best taken with food, immediately after meals, or with a glass of milk to 
  minimize stomach irritation.
- Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to control symptoms.

3. CONTRAINDICATIONS & SAFETY WARNINGS
Do NOT take Ibuprofen if:
- You have an active stomach or peptic ulcer, gastrointestinal bleeding, or perforation.
- You have a history of severe allergic reactions (asthma, bronchospasm, hives) 
  triggered by aspirin or other NSAIDs.
- You suffer from severe heart failure, severe liver cirrhosis, or end-stage kidney disease.
- You are in the third trimester of pregnancy.
- Avoid drinking alcohol during treatment, as it increases the risk of stomach ulceration.

4. POSSIBLE SIDE EFFECTS
- Common (mild): Heartburn, indigestion, nausea, mild stomach cramps, dizziness.
- Serious (seek immediate medical attention): Black tarry stools, vomiting blood, 
  sudden chest pain, difficulty breathing, or swelling of the face, tongue, and throat.
