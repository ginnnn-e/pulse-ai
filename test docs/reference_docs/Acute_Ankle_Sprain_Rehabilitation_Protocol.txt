================================================================================
CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: ACUTE LATERAL ANKLE SPRAIN REHABILITATION
PulseAI Orthopedic & Sports Medicine Clinical Protocol
================================================================================

1. CLINICAL OVERVIEW & MECHANISM
Lateral ligament sprains are the most common musculoskeletal injury in active individuals, 
typically resulting from inversion stress with plantarflexion. The anterior talofibular 
ligament (ATFL) is damaged first, followed by the calcaneofibular ligament (CFL).

2. PHASE 1: ACUTE MANAGEMENT (FIRST 48-72 HOURS) — THE P.O.L.I.C.E. PROTOCOL
- Protection: Apply an elastic tubular bandage or semi-rigid ankle stirrup brace. 
  Avoid rigid casts whenever possible.
- Optimal Loading: Encourage gentle pain-free active range of motion (ankle pumps, 
  tracing the alphabet with toes) starting from Day 1 to promote collagen alignment.
- Ice (Cryotherapy): Apply crushed ice in a towel to the lateral malleolus for 
  15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours during waking hours.
- Compression: Wrap from the base of the toes up to the mid-calf with consistent 
  moderate tension to control edema.
- Elevation: Elevate the foot above the level of the heart whenever resting.

3. ANALGESIA & PAIN MANAGEMENT
- Short-term oral NSAIDs (such as Ibuprofen 400mg three times daily with food) 
  combined with Paracetamol 1000mg up to four times daily offer effective analgesia.
- Avoid prolonged total bed rest; early protected weight-bearing with crutches 
  hastens return to normal walking.

4. RED FLAGS & EMERGENCY CRITERIA (OTTAWA ANKLE RULES)
Obtain plain radiographs or seek urgent medical review if:
- Inability to bear weight immediately after the injury and in the clinic (4 steps).
- Bone tenderness along the posterior 6cm of the lateral or medial malleolus.
- Severe pain at the base of the 5th metatarsal or navicular bone.
- Progressive numbness or tingling across the top of the foot or toes.
